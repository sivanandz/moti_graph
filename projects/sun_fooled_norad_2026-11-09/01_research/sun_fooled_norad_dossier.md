## 1. Logline & Cold-Open Hook

**Logline**: On May 23, 1967, a colossal solar radio burst blinded all three of America's Ballistic Missile Early Warning System radars at once. In Cold War doctrine, jamming meant Soviet attack — so NORAD moved nuclear-armed bombers toward launch. The attack that nearly triggered a war came not from Moscow, but from a sunspot. And the man who stopped it was a weather forecaster named Arnold Snyder.

**Cold-open hook**: Three radar screens at three sites — Alaska, Greenland, England — go dark in the same minute. The military does not wonder *what* happened. It wonders *who*. Teletype clatters. It types: JAMMING = WAR. Then one line undercuts it: *"No bomber ever launched. The culprit was 93 million miles away."*

The story stayed classified for nearly 50 years, until the 2016 declassification paper by Delores Knipp et al. in AGU's *Space Weather* journal.

## 5. Visual Notes — Tactile Retro-Digital Ephemera

- **Ground layer**: Cream deckle cardstock **#F4EFE6** — visible paper fiber, deckle edges, subtle ink bleed on every card. Cards enter with stepped stop-motion (12–15 fps, spring `stiffness: 220, damping: 14`).
- **Typography**: 1-bit coarse-dithered serif for display; **active typewriter cursor** (`_`) blinking at the last typed character of every headline and teletype line; Courier-style monospace for technical stamps.
- **Ink system**: **Dual-ribbon red/black** — black ink for narrative lines, red ribbon (#DE3831 family) for stamps ("JAMMING", "ACT OF WAR", "STAND DOWN", "DECLASSIFIED"), underline accents and strikethroughs. Red used sparingly: only where the military stakes escalate.

  4. Teletype re-print (forecast bulletin)

- **Motion cadence**: Hybrid — 12–15 fps stepped for archival cutouts and paper slips; smooth 30 fps only for signal traces (radar sweeps, burst curve, Dst spike).
- **Texture details**: photocopy grain on military stamps; visible ribbon-repeat doubling on typed words; red-stamp ink slightly offset from black type (registration error); 1-bit dithering on all photographic cutouts so NASA/NARA photos read as printed ephemera.
